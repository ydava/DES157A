Post 2 - Overlays & Lightboxes

Post 2 • Overlays & Lightboxes

What I Learned

From reading the Nielsen Norman Group article on overlays and lightboxes, I learned that overlays can be useful for focusing user attention on specific tasks or content. However, they are often overused in ways that negatively impact the user experience. When used poorly, overlays can feel intrusive, especially if they appear unexpectedly or are difficult to close.

I also learned that good overlay design requires restraint. They should only be used for important or focused tasks such as confirmations, sign-ins, or form completion. They must also be easy to dismiss, accessible with keyboard controls, and should never trap users or block access to important content.

Overall, overlays are not bad by design, but they need to be carefully implemented to avoid disrupting the user’s flow and sense of control.

Nielsen Norman Group – Overuse of Overlays

Observations

I noticed that many websites use overlays in ways that interrupt the user experience, such as pop-ups that appear immediately when entering a site. These often feel annoying and make me want to leave the page.

I also observed that the best overlays are the ones that feel optional and easy to close. When an overlay is clearly labeled and does not block too much content, it feels much less frustrating.

From my own experience, I prefer interfaces that use inline content or simpler page layouts instead of forcing interactions through pop-ups, because it makes the experience feel smoother and more in control.
